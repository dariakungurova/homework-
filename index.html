Reading

Psychologists often describe personality using a model called the Big Five. This model says that most people's personality includes five main traits, or characteristics. Every person has each trait in a different amount — from very low to very high.

The first trait is openness. People with high openness enjoy new ideas and experiences. They are curious and creative, and they like to try different things.

The second trait is conscientiousness. Conscientious people are organized and responsible. They plan carefully, and they usually finish tasks on time.

The third trait is extraversion. Extraverted people feel comfortable around other people. They enjoy social situations, and they often have a lot of energy. In contrast, introverted people prefer quiet time alone.

The fourth trait is agreeableness. Agreeable people are kind and cooperative. They care about other people's feelings, and they try to avoid conflict.

The fifth trait is neuroticism. This trait describes how people react to stress. People with high neuroticism often feel worried or anxious. People with low neuroticism usually stay calm, even in difficult situations.

Psychologists use these five traits to understand why people behave differently. No trait is "good" or "bad" — each one simply shows a different way of thinking, feeling, and acting.
